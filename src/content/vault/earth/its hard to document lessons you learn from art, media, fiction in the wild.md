- best method I've found thus far is in keeping a [[commonplace books|commonplace book]]
- learning strategies differ for fact-books and experience-books, narratives 
- novels are written for experience-based knowledge, transport you into a characters shoes to uncover a story to learn morally and through absorption you get critical analysis. immersion first, the insights come at the end.
- as with our own narratives, [[lived forwards, understood backwards]]
- quick-note the aha moments
- how did it transform me, change my thinking, resonate with me. what touched me. 
- don't look for overarching themes until afterwards
- learning through sympathy rather than detached logical analysis
- I often don't get the most out of stuff I read because I forget (or don't find time) to do the second read-through where the crucial points come into focus
- feel like I miss some of the insights I gain from playing TTRPGS because I lose track of the specifics when that [[theatre of the mind]] disappears. desperate to keep hold of those relationships in part because of the important life lessons Ive learnt and forgotten that exist in that virtual space between us
- even just listing themes covered in a section might be useful alongside the main text as it acts as hint or index to quickly return to that experiential knowledge within you without trying to preemptively put it into words
