- a 3-4 year project
- by a graduate, aimed at academics
- not your ultimate research triumph but a question/series of questions you want to and think you can answer to some degree within the timeframe
- very specific project at the boundary of academic knowledge
- a PHD is not explorative but concentric
- researching one thing slowly, with consistency and resilience	- in conversation with other scholars
	- here's how I'm adding to yours, here's what you missed, etc

---

## the thesis
- a compelling story others want to read
- defensible and watertight
- pre-empts critique
- should be change others can build on

## the original contribution
- new data, evidencing a gap, mismatching results
- new methodology
- other knowledge ported to another context

---

## the process

- structure
		- by monograph / publication / practise
		- assume monograph
		- it can be all about practises if you have a good examiner
		- by practise came from a specific set of traditions in composition/architecture so maybe harder/riskier to do this way
		- by publication
			- thematic set of papers that all answer overarching question
			- eg different case studies on one topic in different settings
			- at least one publication should've made it through to publication in a journal
			- direction led by journals/trends/discipline more
- viva / defence
		- shouldn't be confrontational, it is supposed to be a rewarding focused discussion of your work
		- confirms your expertise in your field
		- shouldn't be any surprises, your supervisory team and you should know and anticipate issues with your work	
- award
	- no real disciplinary agreement on whether assessment is of the thesis or the student
	- it is possible to have artefact as self-contained instructible piece but is riskier as it is inherently ambiguous and harder to make a watertight case than when you can argue it with words
